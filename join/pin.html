About your key file, your PIN and the details

This page runs no outside scripts: your PIN and your browser’s sign-in key are used only here. Three ways in, one check. The first time, or if you lose your key file: on the join page, type your GitHub username, choose a key file and get a one-time code; post it there, signed in to GitHub as that username, on a line of its own; then set a six-digit PIN here. On a new browser: your username, your key file and your PIN, no post. On this browser again: your PIN alone. Every way ends the same: this browser’s own sign-in key signs a one-time challenge from us, and we check that signature and your PIN. You stay signed in for 30 days.

This page runs only our own scripts: the comment box that takes your code runs on the join page, never here, where your PIN is typed and your browser’s key is used.

This browser’s sign-in key is made by your browser and kept in it. It can’t be copied out, not even by our own page, and no other site can use it, so a look-alike site can’t sign in as you with it, and a sign-in caught on the way can’t be used again. We keep only its public half. You can see and remove your browsers on your account page.

Your key file is any private file only you have, of any type, that you have never sent, uploaded or synced anywhere. It is your backup for a new browser. A file that syncs to a cloud service counts as shared, so pick another. It stays on your device: your browser sends only its fingerprint, and we keep a keyed copy of that fingerprint and of your username until you delete your account (see our privacy policy). It must be the exact same file each time. Only choose it on dayonegreat.com.

Your PIN is never stored as you typed it, only as a keyed, salted hash. A stolen device or a stolen key file alone doesn’t sign anyone in. After 3 wrong key files for your username, signing in with a key file pauses for 10 minutes and you get a notice; your browser and a new code still work. After 5 wrong PINs with a browser or with your key file, you get a notice and Aaron is alerted; after 10, that browser or that key file is cancelled. Your GitHub sign-in is never cancelled: a new code always brings you back to your same account, with a new key file and a new PIN. Signing out everywhere cancels your key file and every browser too.
