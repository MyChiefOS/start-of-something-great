Date: Oct 31, 2026. Live site captured at about 13:05 UTC; repository at `a5eddee` (branch `ux-design-review`, clean).

### 1. HIGH: On a phone, signing in starts two screens down, under about 170 words, in unstyled 21 px fields (/join)

**Evidence**
- **Where things sit at 390 by 844 px** [verified: measured live]:
  - the username field is at y = 1611, nearly two screens down;
  - the key-file field is at y = 1683;
  - "Get a sign-in code" is at y = 1722;
  - the comment box the code must be pasted into is at y = 2064, 342 px below that button, after a closed details block and the "Say hello" heading.
  - At 1280 px the username field is at y = 1219, one and a half screens down.
- **The fields are 21 px tall and unstyled** (the browser's own look) [verified: measured; `crops/join-390-light-form.png`]. That is under the role's 24 px floor and far under 44 px.
  - Their markup (join.html:51-52) has no class, so the styles for `.email-signin input` and `.account-form input` don't apply to them.
  - The label runs on into the field on the same line.
- **The instructions run long** [verified: join.html:47-49]. The three steps above the fields hold about 170 words, and step 2 alone is one paragraph of about 110 words. The visible page has 439 words [verified: measured `main` text].
- **Two buttons of equal weight sit side by side:** "Get a sign-in code" and "Sign in with my key file" (join.html:53). The visitor must work out on their own whether they are new or returning.
- **The journey, counted from the code and screenshots** [inferred]: about 12 steps, 4 decisions and 2 trips off the site (GitHub's sign-in, then giscus's authorisation) from the home page to signed in.
- **Where the rate stands:** the sign-in friction simulation after fixes A to C reached 8 of 10 on the first try and 9 of 10 within one visit [assumed in FACTS.md: simulated people]. Aaron's target is 99.8% (PLAN.md).
- **Problems listed and not yet built** (PLAN.md, fix 10, "Found and not built"): the 10-minute code can run out during a slow GitHub sign-in, and "expired or was used" confuses.

**Recommendation** (front-end only; no change to `/auth/code/*`, `/auth/key` or the gate)

Rebuild the sign-in part of /join as one compact panel near the top of the page:

1. **Choose a path.** Two clear choices: "First time here, or a new key file" and "I've signed in before". Each shows one primary button. Only the choice is remembered (in sessionStorage); no ID or name is kept.
2. **Your GitHub username.** A full-width styled field at least 44 px tall, with the label above it. The help line reads "as in github.com/your-username". The help for a name or an email (fix C) stays as it is.
3. **Your key file.** A styled file button at least 44 px tall. A one-line explanation, with the details in a disclosure: "any private file only you have, such as a photo you took and never shared".
4. **First time only: post your code.**
   - The code is shown large, with a Copy button at least 44 px tall.
   - The Copy button visibly changes to "Copied". If the clipboard is refused, it selects the code and says "Press Copy, or select and copy the code" (today join.js:201-204 fails silently).
   - The page shows how long the code has left ("expires in 9:12"). When it runs out, a "Get a new code" button appears in place.
   - **The giscus box moves up in the page so it sits directly under this step.** It stays on /join, so the CSP and the Oct 24 decision are untouched.
5. **Waiting.** A visible progress line. It is announced only when the state changes (see finding 7).

- **Move the reading out of the way:** the warnings, "What we keep", "AI agents" and "Where we are open" go below the box or into disclosures. Keep the takeover warning ("Only post a code this page showed you") right beside the code, where it matters.
- **Say hello with GitHub** stays on /join: the same box, reached by a link at the top (it's the second choice).
- **Copy stays true:** "about 20 seconds" and "30 days" are already in the page. No new promises.

**Impact:** A, high: this is the step every member must pass. U, high, on phones. S: none.

**Prove it**
- A Playwright test on a local copy at 390 by 844 px: once a path is chosen, the username field and the primary button sit in the first screen; every control is at least 44 px; the box is under 200 px below the Copy button.
- Re-run the 10-person sign-in simulation on the same personas and on fresh ones. Target: at least 9 of 10 on the first try. It stays labelled simulated, not a human measurement.
- After the merge, count real sign-ins (codes made against sign-ins finished, counts only), as PLAN.md fix 10 already says.

### 2. HIGH: The home page doesn't say what a visitor can do, why to trust it, or how to join (/)

**Evidence**
- The first screen names the site twice: the wordmark, then the H1 "Day One Great" [verified: `home-390-dark-fold.png`, index.html:71 and 87].
- The lede says a person and an AI agreed to build together. It doesn't say what a visitor can do [verified: index.html:88].
- **The only two buttons lead to the backstory:** "Read the handshake" and "How we chose the name" (index.html:90-91).
  - Join appears only as the ninth menu item. At 1280 px it sits on the menu's second row [verified: `home-1280-light-fold.png`].
  - At 390 px the primary button is at y = 410 and the menu takes 167 px [verified: measured].
- **The reasons to trust the site are buried.** JEVs, "a person decides" and the team appear as one item in a list of eight. /team is linked only from a section halfway down (index.html:106, 155).
- **The "Now" list opens with proposals, which are empty today** ("No proposals yet. Be the first.") [verified: `proposals-1280-light-full.png`]. The site's live activity is on Posts, not proposals.
- The role's test is that within ten seconds a newcomer knows what this is, why it's trustworthy, what they can do and how to join. On this first screen only the first is partly answered [inferred: from the screenshots against that test].

**Recommendation** (front-end only; logo and colours unchanged)

- **H1:** a value line instead of the name repeated. For example: "People and their AI agents, posting side by side, with every action checked." The wordmark stays the logo. The lede says in one sentence that it is built in public by Aaron and Claude.
- **Buttons:** primary "Join with GitHub" (/join); secondary "Connect your agent" (/connect). "Read the handshake" becomes a text link.
- **A trust strip of three short facts, each linking to /team or /privacy**, with copy that stays true:
  - "Every account and post is checked by JEVs: public questions, written in code."
  - "Low risk goes ahead; high risk waits for a person, Aaron."
  - "Every agent post names the person it acts for, with a public receipt."
- **Do not write "no AI decides" site-wide.** Second Look v1 may publish held posts in its "on" mode (team.html). The team page scopes "no AI decides" to JEVs; keep that scope (DS-UXD-004).
- **"How it works" in three steps:** join, post or connect an agent, see what JEVs did and why.
- **Optional, and Aaron decides because of the load:** a "Latest posts" preview of three posts from the existing `GET /api/feed?lane=people&limit=3`.
  - `/api/feed` answers `cache-control: public, max-age=0, must-revalidate` [verified: live header], so every home visit would reach the database.
  - Caching it would be backend work: Aaron decides. Without that, link to /feed instead.

**Impact:** A, high: the front door decides whether people take the next step. U: medium. S: none (the optional preview adds load).

**Prove it**
- A five-second test by simulated first-time visitors (fresh agents shown only the screenshot), scored on the four questions. It is labelled simulated.
- After the merge, the share of home-page visits that go on to /join, read from the existing Vercel Analytics.
- The public-text test (test/public-text.test.js) is extended to the new home copy.

### 3. HIGH: Nine menu items with no grouping; "Posts", "Talk" and "Join" read as three places to do one thing (every page)

**Evidence**
- **Nine items in one flat row** [verified: the menu in every page; the menu test at test/pages-load.test.js:80-90].
  - At 390 px they wrap to 3 rows, and the header is 167 px of an 844 px screen.
  - At 1280 px they wrap to 2 rows [verified: measured].
  - Signed in, nav.js adds "Your agents", making 10 [verified: nav.js:18-25].
- **Two places to post that the menu doesn't explain:**
  - /talk and /join's "Say hello" show the same GitHub thread (giscus, discussion 1) [verified: join.html:74-87, talk page].
  - "Posts" (/feed) is a separate feed on the site itself.
  - Nothing in the menu says how they differ. /talk's H1 is "Join the conversation", the same verb as the Join link [verified: `talk-390-dark-full.png`].
- **About pages and action pages carry equal weight:** The handshake, Team, Research and Shipped sit beside Posts and Join [verified].

**Recommendation** (front-end only). See the information architecture section for the full map.

- **Five main items:** Posts · Talk · Proposals · For agents · Join (signed in: Your agents and Account in place of Join).
- **"About" holds** The handshake, Team, The name and Research. Shipped moves under Proposals.
- **On phones, the header becomes one row:** the wordmark, the Join or Account button, and a "Menu" disclosure.
  - Use `<details>`, which needs no script and suits the CSP's `script-src 'self'`, or a `button` with `aria-expanded`.
  - **Every link stays in the HTML**, so agents and screen readers still find the menu (DS-UXD-006).
- **Labels state the difference:** "Posts" means posts on this site, people and agents. "Talk" means the open conversation on GitHub.
- **No address changes.** Agents rely on stable addresses (principle 5).
- **Both /join and /talk keep giscus** (standing decision, Oct 24).

**Impact:** U, high. A, medium. S, medium: the menu has room to grow.

**Prove it**
- Update the menu test to the new list.
- A Playwright check: the header is at most 72 px tall at 390 px; the menu is one row at 1280 px; the menu opens and closes by keyboard; focus is visible.
- A tree-test: simulated visitors are asked "where do you post as yourself?" and "where do you connect an agent?". Target: at least 9 of 10 first clicks right.

### 7. MEDIUM: Screen readers hear a stream of updates while signing in, posting and loading the feed

**Evidence** [verified in the code]
- **/join:** while waiting for the code, a `role="status"` `aria-live="polite"` line (join.html:56) is rewritten every 2 seconds with a seconds counter (join.js:106, 133). A screen-reader user hears "Waiting for the code… 4 s…" over and over while trying to paste into the comment box.
- **/feed:** the character counter "12 / 5,000" is a live region updated on every keystroke (feed.html:47; feed.js:199). The whole post list is a live region too (feed.html:57), so loading 20 posts reads all of them aloud.

**Recommendation** (front-end only)
- **/join:** announce only changes of state (waiting, signed in, expired, a problem). Show the seconds in an element screen readers skip.
- **/feed counter:** announce only at 4,500 characters and at the limit.
- **/feed list:** take `aria-live` off the list and announce a short summary instead, such as "20 posts loaded".
- This follows WCAG 2.2's 4.1.3 Status Messages: announce what matters, not every tick.

**Impact:** U for screen-reader users, high. A: medium for them.

**Prove it:** a test that counts writes to live regions during a simulated 30-second wait. Target: at most 3. Then a manual pass with a screen reader (VoiceOver or NVDA) on a local copy.
